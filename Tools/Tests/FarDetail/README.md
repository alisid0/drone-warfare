# Detail streaming hand-off

Branch: `codex/detail-streaming`, based on `fc81c6f`. No Unity editor/player was launched or Library imported in this worktree. Claude should integrate on top of the hd136 fixes, rather than replace them with the old base.

The ring replaces coarse geometry with 512 m tiles: 4 m vertices, a 256 x 256 ground texture (2 m/texel), 80 m skirts, and a fixed 128 m morph at tile edges. Tile cores use WorldPlan.Height plus Ground's two exact native Perlin roughness scales, with wet ground suppressed. At the outer boundary the coarse mesh is cut to the rectangle and stitched to the tile's 4 m edge samples. Neighboring detail edges use the same heights regardless of completion order. All tiles use the same mesh resolution; only the surrounding chunks retain their LODs.

The detailed sector and Backdrop retain ownership. Whole tiles intersecting their radial footprint are excluded. Clipped chunks are forced to LOD0 while occupied; their ordinary LODs return on eviction. Mesh buffers are prepared invisibly and swapped with the tile in one main-thread publication. This prevents a frame with an uncovered hole or overlapping top surfaces. Fixed morph bands also mean that full roughness is strongest in each tile's central 256 m square; inspect those bands at low altitude in the player.

Workers consume immutable copies of world grids, vectors and coarse geometry, and output arrays only. Unity's native Perlin inputs are sampled on the main thread under a 0.8 ms stopwatch budget, including the world picture's exact cover drifts. A small native-noise lookup table preserves FarTerrain.Field's strip warp. Main-thread upload stages are spread across frames: positions, normals, UVs, indices, pixel data, Apply, collision mesh, then affected chunk buffers. One tile can publish per frame. Colliders use 16 m vertices within 500 m; Ground.Raycast uses exact tile triangles for accurate clicks at every live distance. Ground.HeightAt samples that same triangle, not a bilinear substitute.

Camera velocity is measured in real seconds, so 4x movement automatically predicts four times the travel. The selected/followed drone provides an independent bubble even when its feed window is hidden. Requests sort by arrival into the protected 500 m area, then by distance only for equal arrivals. The corridor reaches 1.5 km ahead. Camera and drone teleports reset velocity. A world rebuild can discard an old worker without waiting because its captured data has no live scene dependencies.

## Capacity and retention

- Hard cap: 25 visible tiles, plus one hidden pooled staging slot. Meshes, textures, materials and collision meshes are reused.
- The 1.5 km radius is a candidate radius, not a guarantee of a full disk: a disk alone covers 27 tile areas before edge coverage, retention or the second bubble. The cap takes priority; immediate camera/feed coverage and predicted arrival win the available slots. Lateral coverage therefore contracts, particularly with a distant feed.
- Retention is 15 real seconds after leaving the ring, followed by staged retirement. Under cap pressure an unrequested tile outside both 500 m bubbles can retire earlier; `PressureEvictions` records this compromise. Keeping every departed tile for 15 seconds at 4x would require a higher cap.
- Partial tiles at the theatre edge are allowed. Heights hold the world-edge sample; wholly outside tiles are rejected.

## Reproduce the checks

Run `Tools/Tests/FarDetail/run.ps1` from this worktree. It uses the installed Unity .NET SDK and Python 3.13 with `-I`, writes generated scaffolding under `Temp/far-detail`, and creates the review outputs here. It installs no packages and does not launch Unity. Pillow is already available to the supplied Python installation.

The API target compiles complete FarDetail helpers, FarTerrain, FarScatter, Ground and their terrain consumers against Unity 6000.6.7f1's Core/Terrain/TerrainPhysics/Physics/Animation assemblies, with C# 9 and warnings as errors. The modified WorldCheck method, including its local screenshot coroutine, is copied verbatim into an API wrapper. Unrelated game services and HDRP water properties are stubs; this is not a full player build. New engine signatures were checked against the installed XML API documentation as well as these assemblies.

The data target runs the real WorldPlan, tile generation, clipping, ray traversal and streaming state machine. The world-picture painter is extracted verbatim from FarTerrain for comparison. Both painters use the same managed reference Perlin implementation because native Unity Perlin is unavailable outside the player. The screenshots are CPU texture renders, not Unity views. Runtime uses native Perlin for height roughness and cover drift.

Checks cover mesh indices/winding, core heights, edge equality, strip-pattern agreement, rectangle area conservation, absence of coarse triangles under tiles, stitched boundary heights, vertical/slanted/upward rays, immutable snapshots during regeneration, a warmed 20 km flight at 160 m/s, turns, teleports, feed coverage, near colliders, atomic chunk/tile ownership, the 25-tile cap, expiry, LOD restoration and a sector rebuild. The state-machine simulation synchronizes worker completion between virtual frames; it is deliberately not a throughput or FPS benchmark.

## Cost estimates, not player measurements

Each tile has 17,157 vertices including skirts, 34,816 triangles, 1.21 MiB of managed output arrays, and about 1.06 MiB of GPU mesh/texture payload including mipmaps. The 26-slot pool is about 27.5 MiB of tile payload when fully populated. Clipped chunks retain shared vertices rather than duplicating every triangle; the review cases need about 4,400-5,500 vertices each. Their double buffers are released when a chunk has no detail tiles. A normal camera/feed ring should remain well below 50 MiB of additional GPU payload; a conservative dispersed-tile allowance is below 120 MiB, excluding the existing far-world resources and driver overhead. Verify actual VRAM in the player against the 200 MB ring gate.

The largest tile upload source is the 408 KiB index array; the texture is 256 KiB plus mip generation. At an assumed effective 2 GiB/s copy rate, the largest transfer alone is about 0.2 ms. Allow roughly 0.2-0.8 ms for an ordinary staged commit including native-call/driver overhead. These are planning estimates only. MeshCollider cooking, texture Apply, Unity's allocator and first-use graphics work are unmeasured and non-preemptible. The stopwatch prevents stacking stages but cannot forcibly interrupt a native call at 2 ms. WorldCheck logs the real mean, worst and >2 ms count and fails the gate if those limits are exceeded. Do not treat the harness timings as proof of the 1 ms average / 2 ms maximum requirement.

## Claude's build checklist

1. Integrate the terrain/data helpers and their `.meta` files, then the narrow hooks. Preserve current hd136 Ground fixes outside HeightAt/Raycast. FarTerrain's hook is Clear before rebuilding, Register for each completed LOD0 chunk, Initialise afterward, and rejection of old collider hits under a live tile.
2. Reconcile FarScatter with the other Codex branch through its small `GroundHeight(local, tree)` helper. The two placement calls use it. **The `tree=true` contract cancels this base revision's subsequent `CanopyAt - 9` lift. If the forest-tier branch removes that lift, use `FarDetail.ScatterHeight(local, false)` for tree ground too.** Do not apply the compensation twice. A changed macro canopy height is automatically captured from the rebuilt LOD0 meshes.
3. Make a normal Unity 6000.6.7f1 HDRP build. Check for script/compiler errors, missing materials, missing tangents warnings, worker-thread engine calls and texture/collider errors. Use the normal player build pipeline; no setup/package changes are required.
4. Run the autotest at **1920 x 1080 on the RTX 3050 laptop**, with graphics settings and VSync/frame cap recorded. WorldCheck's existing Swift flight now follows continuously at low zoom at 4x and takes `86_world_low_detail` and `87_world_low_forest_edge` during the flight. Each shot logs its actual 40-60 m clearance, location and live-tile status. It searches within 1,536 m of the current flight position; a missing requested cover or timeout is a reported test error, not a silently substituted view.
5. Inspect the `World detail flight` log: live <=25, committed >0, main-thread mean <=1 ms, worst <=2 ms, zero >2 ms frames, and frame-time p95 <16.7 ms. Resolution and GPU name are included. Screenshot I/O is excluded from the p95 sample; streaming, low-view camera movement and warmup waits are included. Preserve the profiler capture, not just average FPS.
6. In the CPU/GPU profiler, inspect SetVertices/SetIndices, Texture2D.Apply, MeshCollider cooking, activation, garbage collection and HDRP draw cost separately. Check incremental ring VRAM after a 20 km flight, a return trip, and a sector rebuild. If a native stage exceeds 2 ms, the gate is not met even if the average is low. Actual GPU/frame-time and commit gates remain unverified here.
7. Fly across at least ten 512 m boundaries and a 4 km chunk boundary at 40 m/s, 40-60 m AGL, then repeat at 4x and descend to 30-50 m. Inspect skirts, morph bands, canopy edges, field alignment, true-width roads and a river crossing. Check for geometry/texture pop; fixed edge bands and absence of blank geometry do not themselves prove visually smooth activation.
8. Click/issue orders on both near and distant live tiles, including oblique rays and chunk/tile edges. Verify the point remains on the ray and visible ground. Check houses and tree roots after tile arrival/eviction; cached scatter uses the eventual triangle height before activation to avoid repeatedly rebuilding every scatter cell.
9. Separate the main camera and selected drone by several kilometres, show/hide the feed, turn sharply, pause, teleport through the atlas and return within 15 seconds. Check both protected bubbles, correct priorities, pressure-eviction counts and refill behavior. Rapid teleports can require a refill; this is asynchronous streaming, not preloaded scenery everywhere.
10. Return into the detailed sector, wait at least 20 real seconds, and confirm old tiles retire and empty chunks recover normal LODs. Change sector/world while a tile is sampling, generating and uploading; there must be no stale tile, permanent clipped hole, retained collider or resource leak.
