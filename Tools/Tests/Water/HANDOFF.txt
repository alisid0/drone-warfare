Water review for Claude -- codex/water, based on da49170

The river now uses stronger HDRP ambient scattering, a shorter absorption length,
and glossy ripples that persist across the strategy camera's slant distance. HDRP
still supplies Fresnel, sky/SSR reflections, shadows and sun highlights. No custom
shader, extra water surface, reflection pass, simulation, or per-frame script was
added. The existing material remains the renderer; no custom-shader fallback is
needed. The separate lake and pond surface settings are unchanged.

Varrow's river and plunge pool share a WaterSurface, so its scattering changes
also affect the plunge pool. Its wave amplitudes and spillway foam are retained.
The dam's close view MUST be compared in Unity; the Blender reviews cannot prove
that it remains as convincing. All times of day and actual frame time are also
unverified until the integration build runs.

Far water uses a shared grey-blue/green palette and physical shallow-water bands.
The tile, regional and final world picture share this painter. Lake polygons own
their true shore instead of the 32 m water-cover raster. This also removes the
old dry ribbon through the middle of lakes where river-cover suppression erased
the lake. River mouths stay open through the shoreline band. The sector colour
collar no longer overwrites water with land colours. Texture sizes and residency
budgets are unchanged.

The far shore cache stores signed distances on a small 16 m grid per lake. A
conservative distance bound skips polygon work only where colour/coverage cannot
change; the band itself remains exact. The 1024-square lake-heavy paint was
byte-identical with and without the cache:

Protected-file patches (not applied in this worktree)

1. Claude-Ground-water.patch
   - Builds an immutable WatercourseProfile after the water levels are known.
   - Narrows/widens within 80-100% of the existing channel envelope over broad
     180/270 m patterns; pools are deeper than riffles. WorldPlan data is not
     changed. The far painter uses the same width rule at river stations.
   - Makes 1-4 m shelves wider on inner bends and outer banks steeper. Roads,
     building pads, lake inlets and the existing dam gorge are protected.
   - Adds 1-2 m wet darkening, pale gravel and a 5 m reservoir draw-down strip;
     natural lakes/ponds get narrower 2.5 m margins. Islands use signed shores too.
   - Reuses the dirt texture for terrain layer 6 (wet mud) and 7 (shore gravel).
     The six-layer terrain already uses HDRP's eight-layer shader variant, so
     this does not introduce another terrain pass. Peak splat staging grows by
     32 MiB (2048 x 2048 x 2 floats); confirm actual GPU cost in Unity.
   - FarDetailEdge handles either the current six layers or these eight layers.
     Keep layer numbers 0-5 unchanged, especially litter at 5.
   - One Ground.PaintForestFloor mask line also respects layers 6/7 so subsequent
     litter painting cannot erase the exposed banks. Reconcile that hunk with
     Codex A's work; no tree placement or forest visibility code is changed.

2. Claude-Grass-water.patch
   - Keeps existing instanced reeds and their distance/density limits.
   - Uses the same waterline, favouring slow, broad-margin reaches.
   - Keeps grass off the exposed river bars and lake draw-down strip.
   - No forest files, canopy logic, Atmosphere or World.BuildWoods edits.

Apply both after integrating this branch. Check them with:
  git apply --check --ignore-space-change Tools/Tests/Water/Claude-Ground-water.patch
  git apply --check --ignore-space-change Tools/Tests/Water/Claude-Grass-water.patch
They were generated against da49170's protected files and compiled as temporary
copies. Do not replace the whole Ground.cs/Grass.cs with those copies: reconcile
the small hunks with any newer integration changes. ground_patch.py can regenerate
them from the checked-out protected sources and asserts its replacement context.

The terrain patch is required for the new physical banks/depths and near shore
paint; the branch itself does not modify Ground.cs. The water mesh remains wide
enough for the terrain to define its shoreline. Existing movement and placement
exclusions use the larger surveyed envelope, so they stay conservative.

Unity build checklist

1. Integrate commits and reconcile/apply the Ground and Grass patches. Let Unity
   compile the real HDRP assembly and import the three new scripts/metas. Check
   the log for errors, missing layers, and pink water/terrain.
2. Repeat 02c_bridge at 100/200/400 m and 30/45/60 degrees. Inspect 10:00, dawn,
   dusk and night. Expect visible grey-green water, pale margins, a darker
   channel, sky response and a glint when the sun is behind the water. Check a
   shaded tree reach as well as open water, and High/Medium/Low quality.
3. Compare 20_photo_dam_morning and 25_photo_lake_shore directly with hd149 and
   hd139. Reject a loss of reflection, blown-out foam, milky plunge-pool water,
   disconnected spillway water, or new seams at the gorge protection boundary.
4. At the bend, confirm the inner gravel shelf is broader and the outer bank
   steeper; inspect the last 1-2 m of damp ground. Confirm reeds remain in slow
   margins and grass does not cover the draw-down strip. Check pond/lake edges
   and reservoir islands, plus bridge approaches and road ownership.
5. Repeat 80/81/83 and a 2.5 km reservoir view. Check true narrow river widths,
   continuous lake shores/mouths, tile/regional/world transitions, and water
   crossing the sector collar. Check world Reservoir and LakeDistrict sectors.
6. Compare fixed-camera CPU/GPU timings with the baseline, including water-filled
   views and streaming across lake tiles. Confirm no frame-rate regression;
   no additional water draw/simulation passes should appear. Check splat-build
   memory (+32 MiB staging only) on the constrained machine. The 32 MiB may be
   avoided later with row-banded splat construction, outside this patch's scope.
7. Keep the existing autotest streaming log checks and run the normal autotest.
