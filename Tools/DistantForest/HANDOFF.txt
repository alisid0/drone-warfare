Confirmed code defects and fixes:

1. ForestFade read input.positionRWS in every raster pass. HDRP's LitDepthPass
   omits that varying for ordinary opaque materials, and VaryingMesh initializes
   the missing value to zero. Canopy then measures distance zero from the camera
   in depth, while colour passes measure real distance. That clips away canopy
   depth and breaks colour passes using depth-equal shading. Dithered opaque
   trunk depth also disagreed with colour. ForestLit now requires world position
   before the pass varying layouts are declared. Dither noise also no longer
   relies on a large-coordinate sine hash, and complete coverage skips the hash.

2. The four ShaderLab Properties were ALREADY present at 9f8a34b. Missing
   declarations were not the defect. Their HLSL values were outside
   UnityPerMaterial, making the shader incompatible with the SRP Batcher layout.
   They now share HDRP's UnityPerMaterial buffer through ForestLitProperties.hlsl.
   Explicit RenderMeshInstanced/RenderMesh calls retain per-camera property
   blocks; no global SRP Batcher or GPU Resident Drawer setting is changed.

3. The first World.Build could create a local FarScatter before FarTerrain
   created its persistent one. Both could submit forests. The local renderer
   now yields ownership while FarTerrain is active. Clear now invalidates its
   adopted version, and uploads copy the prepared-data descriptor instead of
   consuming/clearing SectorForest.PreparedCanopy. A later Clear or replacement
   renderer can therefore re-adopt the same completed canopy.

4. Ready used to mean an index array had merely been allocated, and a camera
   could cache cells while sector ownership was incomplete. Ready is now
   published after stems/indices are complete, with a new version. Rendering
   waits for Ready. Cell records are published atomically after their iterator
   finishes. The 120 m/s readiness ramp is gone: actual completed cells determine
   readiness, with a 16 m movement guard around the nominal 350 m tree bubble.
   Missing trees keep canopy coverage rather than opening an empty bubble.

5. Coarse HLOD previously paid for dense perimeter samples and detailed crown
   textures, and a fine build could block newly visible coverage. Missing blocks
   now receive a small 256 m bootstrap mesh/texture first. Temporary skirts hide
   coarse/fine edge mismatches. Missing coverage preempts refinement on a camera
   turn as well as a teleport. Refined 8/16/64/128 m meshes keep a common 8 m
   perimeter and matching normals. Geometry within 256 m tiles adapts around the
   camera; nearby textures are 260 square instead of 516 square. Existing blocks
   crossfade into replacements while coarse coverage remains available.

6. FarScatter walked, copied and sorted cached tree records and multiplied all
   draw matrices every camera/frame. Generation and selection now run in small
   timed slices. One-metre cost bins replace sorting. Completed camera batches
   submit cached arrays directly; the guard and both sides of every transition
   count against the tree budgets. Camera jumps discard stale bubble selections.
   Nearby cells are retained for multiple observers, with a bounded cache.

7. Canopy.Blocks sampled every metre with nine spatial lookups per sample, even
   above all tree crowns. It now indexes overlapping columns, skips empty or
   vertically disjoint spans and preserves the original metre samples and
   consecutive-hit rule for possible intersections. This is a demonstrated CPU
   reduction, not proof that it alone caused hd145's player-frame regression.

Ruled out or hardened:

- The installed HDRP/Core sources apply camera translation to UNITY_MATRIX_M
  for ordinary, regular-instanced AND DOTS-instanced variants. GetObjectToWorldMatrix
  returns that matrix. GetAbsolutePositionWS on its translation, and on
  positionRWS, restores absolute Unity scene positions exactly once. Comparing
  these with cam.transform.position is correct; adding/removing another camera
  translation would introduce a bug. The existing pivot conversion is retained.
- All 12 Resources/Materials/Forest assets already retained instancing variants.
  They remain serialized with instancing enabled and the foliage cutout/double-
  sided keywords. Runtime clones now use those assets without swapping shaders.
- ForestMaterials checks Shader.Find, isSupported, properties and material/MPB
  value storage. Failures use the existing HDRP Lit PBR/BaseLit assets, with
  instancing enabled and no custom fade. ForceFallback permits a build test.
  Shader fading defaults off. Material camera/distance defaults are zero after
  validation; if a backend drops the property block, the shader draws full
  coverage instead of clipping everything. CPU readback cannot prove GPU binding.
- World.TreeCount counts decorative GameObject trees, not logical sector stems.
  A lower World.TreeCount alone is not evidence that the forest is missing.
